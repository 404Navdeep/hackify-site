01 the tool

Your music player is a
website wearing a trenchcoat.

Spotify's desktop client isn't some sealed native binary. It's HTML, CSS and JavaScript — the exact same stuff a webpage is made of — just shipped in a box with the lid glued shut.

Spicetify is an open-source command-line tool that unglues the lid. It patches the client so your stylesheet and your script load right next to Spotify's, every single time you hit play. No browser extension. No sketchy mod. Just your code, inside the app you already use every day.

If you can style a <div>, you can restyle Spotify.

~/spicetify
$ spicetify config current_theme mine
$ spicetify apply
✔ your css is now spotify's css

$ # regret it?
$ spicetify restore
✔ nothing was harmed

one command puts it all back →

  1. .css

    Themes

    A stylesheet plus a colour palette. Repaint every pixel — corners, fonts, spacing, the lot.

  2. .js

    Extensions

    A script with full access to the player. React to track changes, add buttons, automate things.

  3. /app

    Custom apps

    An entire page of your own, living in the sidebar next to Home and Search. Yes, really.

  4. ~snip

    Snippets

    Three lines of CSS that fix the one thing that's annoyed you for four years. Counts too.

06 liner notes

Questions you were
about to ask.

I've never touched Spicetify. Am I cooked?

No. If you've ever written CSS, you can make a decent theme in an afternoon — you're editing a stylesheet, that's it. The docs are genuinely good, and #hackify is full of people who were also confused about 40 minutes ago.

Do I need Premium already to build this?

Nope. Spicetify runs on the free Spotify desktop client. Building your thing costs you nothing but time — and time is the currency here.

Does it really stack, or is there a secret cap?

It really stacks. 2 hours is 1 month. 10 hours is 5 months. 30 hours is 15 months. The maths does not get more complicated than dividing by two.

What actually counts as a Hackify project?

Anything that changes Spotify through Spicetify: a theme, an extension, a custom app, or a set of snippets that together do something real. If you're squinting at your idea wondering whether it counts, post it in #hackify and someone will tell you it does.

Am I going to permanently destroy my Spotify?

spicetify restore. That's the whole answer. It puts the client back exactly how it was, and you can re-apply whenever you want. Break things freely.

Windows, Mac, or Linux?

All three. Spicetify supports the desktop client on every one of them. (The Microsoft Store build of Spotify is the one that sulks — grab the installer from spotify.com instead.)

Is Spotify running this?

No. Hackify is a Hack Club YSWS. Spicetify is an independent open-source project made by the community. Spotify has nothing to do with either of us — we're just extremely fond of their app's DOM.

What's a YSWS? What's Hack Club?

YSWS stands for You Ship, We Ship: you make something real and send it in, we send something real back. Hack Club is a nonprofit and a network of 100,000+ teenage hackers who learn by building things that mostly work.
